# Project management and review

Project management connects canonical project plans to assignments, execution, review, decisions, and sustainable capacity. It is the durable coordination layer above individual provider runs.

## Commitment boundary

Ideas, conversations, and generated proposals remain exploratory until an explicit accepted change creates or updates Projects data. Commitment records the objective, scope, acceptance criteria, owner, reviewer, dates, dependencies, budget, and expected output appropriate to the work.

See [Guided planning](guided-planning.md) for the exploration and acceptance workflow.

## Assignment

An assignment links a task or explicit objective to a stable participant. AI teammate identity is separate from provider and model choice. Every executable assignment resolves project, conversation, context package, authority, execution target, provider capability, budget, and review destination before work starts.

A participant cannot delegate authority it does not hold. Child assignments retain their own objective, authority, budget, and result link.

## Review

Review records the proposed or completed result, changed artifacts, assumptions, exceptions, decisions, and requested follow-up. Detailed provider events remain in the linked run timeline; project review surfaces emphasize material outcomes.

Approval of one result does not grant standing approval for future actions. Rejected and superseded proposals remain understandable in history.

## Decisions and requirements

Requirements and decisions retain rationale, alternatives, author, time, and affected work. Later changes append history instead of overwriting why the prior state existed.

Accepted outcomes update typed Project records. Chat messages, generated reports, and provider output are context, not substitutes for canonical task or decision state.

## Capacity and anti-burnout

Planning considers estimates, existing commitments, uncertainty, focus capacity, recovery time, and explicit deadlines. It can identify overload and propose scope or date changes without grading the user or manufacturing urgency.
